# Win Pakistan with reports, not ads

LiveHealthy: Vitals already matches the leading indie vital-sign loggers on features. Its real advantages are an Urdu interface, BP, glucose, SpO2 and weight in one app, and a shared account with Medicine Reminder. So v1.1 should lead with a free doctor report (PDF and CSV, shared through WhatsApp), guideline-based averages, and accessibility fixes. Ads should come in quietly alongside these, not as the headline. The market evidence is consistent: users praise fast entry and reports their doctor can read, and ratings drop when apps put export behind a paywall, take away free features, or show ads that block logging. AdMob can go in compliantly with little engineering: one anchored adaptive banner on the History and Charts screens only, non-personalized ads for everyone, Google's UMP consent flow, no health signal ever reaching an ad request, and an updated Data safety form, privacy policy and app-ads.txt. The money will be small. At Pakistan banner rates, 1,000 daily users bring in roughly **$15–55 a month**. Treat ads as portfolio consistency and a reason to sell a cheap one-time "Remove ads" unlock, not as a business model. The release has two hard blockers that are not code: **app-ads.txt currently returns 404 on livehealthy.homilabs.org**, and the **shared privacy policy says the apps contain no ad SDKs**. Both must be fixed before an ads build ships.

## Vitals sits at feature parity in a market with no Urdu rival

The manual-entry logbook category is led by long-running indie apps. "Blood Pressure / My Heart" has **10M+ installs and a 4.3 rating from 56.9K reviews**, and SmartBP, MyDiary and mEL Studio each have 1M+. Device-maker apps such as OMRON connect and diabetes platforms such as mySugr sit beside them ([My Heart](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.szyk.myheart); [SmartBP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.smartbloodpressure); [OMRON](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.omronhealthcare.omronconnect)). These leaders already ship family profiles, PDF and spreadsheet export, tags, reminders and Health Connect sync. My Heart now also offers 2025 AHA/ACC classification, camera scanning of the monitor, Google Drive auto-backup and export to AI chatbots ([My Heart](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.szyk.myheart)). Vitals' v1.0 set of multi-vital entry, colour flags, 7/30/90-day charts, profiles and reminders is therefore table stakes, not a moat. The gaps it can exploit are narrower. Few loggers combine SpO2 with BP and glucose: MyDiary does, but it has not been updated since **September 2023** ([MyDiary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zlamanit.blood.pressure)). The research found no established Urdu-language competitor. A Play search for "urdu blood pressure" on the Pakistan storefront returned only generic English apps ([Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=urdu+blood+pressure&c=apps&gl=pk)). The one Urdu-first caregiver app, Khayal by ISRA, launched in November 2026 with **1+ installs**, offering Urdu voice logging, caregiver linking and WhatsApp summaries ([Khayal](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.isra.android)).

The need is large. Pakistan has the **world's highest adult diabetes prevalence: 31.4% age-standardised, about 34.5M adults** ([IDF Diabetes Atlas](https://diabetesatlas.org/data-by-location/country/pakistan)). About **42% of adults aged 30–79 have hypertension, and only 12% have it controlled** ([The News, WHO report](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1345934-nearly-42pc-pakistanis-hypertensive-only-12pc-control-it-who)). The phone base is **91.6% Android** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile-tablet-console/pakistan)), with versions spread from Android 11 to Android 16 ([StatCounter versions](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-tablet/pakistan)). WhatsApp reaches about **91.7M active users** ([Samaa TV, PTA figures](https://adminpanel.samaa.tv/2087338556-social-media-users-in-pakistan-cross-51-6m-facebook-most-popular)). Discoverability is a bigger risk than competition, so Urdu store listing text, Urdu screenshots and Roman-Urdu keywords ("sugar", "BP") probably matter as much as any feature. Roman Urdu remains the dominant texting style in Pakistan ([Roman-txt, MobileHCI 2017](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/roman-txt-forms-and-functions-of-roman-urdu-texting/)).

Competitor reviews show what users want. Praise centres on speed ("input my numbers quickly, save and close") and on reports for the doctor ([MyDiary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zlamanit.blood.pressure)). The most common complaint is losing data when changing phones, which appears in reviews of My Heart, MyDiary and Glucose Buddy ([My Heart](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.szyk.myheart); [Glucose Buddy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.skyhealth.glucosebuddyfree)). Vitals' Firebase account sync already solves this, so the store listing should say so plainly: "change phones, keep your readings". Other recurring complaints are vitals that can be entered but are never charted ([MyDiary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zlamanit.blood.pressure)), clumsy PDF layouts ([mEL BP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=melstudio.mpresssure)), and fake "measure with your finger" apps that erode trust in the whole category ([Oxylab](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.blood.sugar.pressure.monitor.app)).

## Competitors lose ratings by taking free features away, not by having ads

Almost every leader is freemium: ads plus a subscription or lifetime unlock that removes ads and adds extras. What separates healthy ratings from collapsed ones is fairness. A MyDiary reviewer called the ads "not bad" and paid to remove them once the doctor liked the reports ([MyDiary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zlamanit.blood.pressure)). Glucose Buddy stopped generating reports in its free tier, a reviewer refused to pay "$20/month", and the app now rates **1.8** ([Glucose Buddy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.skyhealth.glucosebuddyfree)). Diabetes:M and mySugr drew lasting anger by replacing one-time purchases with subscriptions ([Diabetes:M](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mydiabetes); [mySugr](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mysugr.android.companion)). The most damaging ad pattern is the one that blocks logging. Oxylab's ad-only tracker draws reviews like "ads always covering the screen" and "nigh on impossible due to the ads" ([Oxylab](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.blood.sugar.pressure.monitor.app)), and a SmartBP user "can't get past ads" at launch ([SmartBP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.smartbloodpressure)). Two rules follow for Vitals. Nothing free in v1.0 may become paid. The new doctor report should also stay free, because export paywalls are a recurring source of one-star reviews in this category ([mEL BP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=melstudio.mpresssure)).

Older users make the ad question harder. Older adults are worse than younger ones at recognising native ads ([CogSci 2020](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/54h18974)). **49% of baby boomers rarely click a mobile ad on purpose, and 24% never do** ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b23d8933-4f9b-4850-a9cd-71d3005c6f23)). That makes accidental taps a likely share of any clicks, and Google treats accidental taps as invalid traffic. Google itself names banners placed next to buttons or navigation as a leading cause of accidental clicks ([AdMob banner guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6275345)). A second risk is that a supplement or "cure diabetes" ad shown beside a red flag could look like the app's own advice. That is why the ad design below keeps health-product categories blocked and keeps ads off every screen that shows a result.

## The v1.1 roadmap leads with a free doctor report

Effort estimates follow the feature research's scale for one developer: **S is about one week or less, M is 2–4 weeks, and L is more than a month or needs hardware or a policy review**. The ranking weighs value for elderly users and caregivers in Pakistan against effort and risk.

| Release | Item | Effort | Why now / main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v1.1** | Doctor report: PDF + CSV, shared through the Android share sheet (WhatsApp), free | S–M | Most-praised competitor feature. Urdu Nastaliq shaping in the `pdf` package is unconfirmed, so fall back to an English PDF |
| v1.1 | Statistics: averages, min/max, morning vs evening, time in range, trend vs previous period, plus a "Doctor's 7-day BP check" | S–M | Guideline-backed and pure Dart. Wording must say "suggests, discuss with your doctor" |
| v1.1 | Context tags: before/after medicine on BP, symptom chips, notes | S | Cheap, and doctors use them |
| v1.1 | Accessibility pass: 200% font scale, 3:1 chart and flag contrast, save confirmation with undo, plausibility warnings, TalkBack value + unit + status | S | Low risk, directly serves elderly users |
| v1.1 | AdMob banner + UMP + compliance paperwork (see next section) | S (~1 week incl. Console and policy work) | Policy and trust, not code |
| v1.1 | Urdu store listing, Roman-Urdu keywords, "change phones, keep readings" message | S | Discoverability is the main growth constraint |
| **v1.2** | One-time "Remove ads" in-app purchase, entitlement restored before the ad SDK starts | S | Gives ad-averse caregivers an exit |
| v1.2 | Caregiver sharing: invite code into `memberUids`, viewer/editor roles | M | Firestore rules shared with Medicine Reminder, so the sibling app must not break |
| v1.2 | Medicine Reminder link: adherence overlaid on BP chart and in the PDF | M | No competitor has it. Depends on matching profile IDs across apps |
| v1.2 | Flutter 3.44+ upgrade, then Health Connect read/write | M–L | Needs Play review. Health Connect data must never reach ads |
| v1.2 | Home-screen widget with a "hide values" option, offline sync indicator, JSON backup | S–M | Medium value |
| **Later** | Caregiver push alerts for very high readings (Cloud Functions + FCM) | M | New server infrastructure. Needs a "not an emergency service" disclaimer |
| Later | Voice entry in English/Urdu | M | Urdu recognizer availability varies by device |
| Later | Camera OCR of the monitor display | M–L | No proven ML Kit accuracy on 7-segment digits |
| Later | Bluetooth monitors and scales | L | Paid licence for `flutter_blue_plus`. Few local monitors have BLE |
| **Avoid** | "Estimated HbA1c" | — | Clinically misleading |

The report is first because it turns logging into something doctors act on. Reports from competing apps are praised for saving handwriting at clinic visits ([MyDiary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zlamanit.blood.pressure)). The `pdf` package (v3.13.1) supports multi-page tables, SVG and TrueType fonts ([pub.dev pdf](https://pub.dev/packages/pdf)), so the whole report can be built on the device with nothing sent to a server. The report should centre on the home BP average that clinicians recognise. The ESH 2021 protocol takes two readings morning and evening and discards day 1 ([PMC9532917](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9532917/)), and a home average of **135/85 mmHg or higher suggests hypertension** ([RACGP](https://www.racgp.org.au/afp/2016/januaryfebruary/how-to-measure-home-blood-pressure-recommendations-for-healthcare-professionals-and-patients/)). The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline also emphasises home monitoring to confirm and tailor treatment ([ACC](https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2025/08/13/20/03/new-high-blood-pressure-guideline-emphasizes-prevention-early-treatment-to-reduce-cvd-risk)). Doing this well answers a user request competitors ignore, to be told "if the average is good or not" ([mEL BP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=melstudio.mpresssure)). It fits Vitals' existing green/amber/red flags.

The accessibility pass targets known gaps rather than repeating what v1.0 already did. Android 14 scales text nonlinearly up to **200%**, and Flutter recommends testing at that maximum, not at 1.3x ([Flutter docs](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/android-14-nonlinear-text-scaling-migration)). WCAG 2.2 requires **3:1 contrast for non-text elements** such as chart lines and flag chips, a common failure for amber on white ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Touch targets need at least 48dp with 8dp spacing ([Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). Research on older adults' app design largely neglects text entry and button feedback ([JMIR mHealth 2023](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e43186)), and logging a reading is exactly that, so the keypad and save confirmation are where careful work pays off.

Health Connect waits for v1.2 because of the toolchain. The current `health` 14.0.0 package requires **Flutter 3.44+ and Dart 3.12+**, newer than Vitals' Flutter 3.41.9 / Dart 3.11. It also needs compileSdk 37, AGP 9.1, and `MainActivity` changed to `FlutterFragmentActivity` ([pub.dev health](https://pub.dev/packages/health)). Play also requires per-data-type justification in the Health apps declaration ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish)). Its policy forbids using health data for ads or sending it to ad platforms ([Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)). Building the ads layer in v1.1 with a hard wall between ads and readings makes that later review easier. Bluetooth is last for three reasons: `flutter_blue_plus` needs a paid commercial licence for for-profit apps ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_blue_plus)), many monitors sold locally have no Bluetooth, and Health Connect delivers most of the benefit through makers' own apps.

## Ads go in as one banner, non-personalized, walled off from readings

Three policies set the boundaries. Google's publisher policies forbid targeting personalized ads on health or medical information, including data from apps that market to a health-related group, and forbid passing anything Google could recognise as personal information ([Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437486)). The AdMob interstitial rules ban interstitials on app load or exit, after every user action, and when the user is "focused on a task at hand" such as filling a form ([AdMob interstitial policy](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362)). Saving a reading is exactly such a task. Play's Ads policy forbids ads that imitate app UI and unexpected full-screen ads ([Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)). Together these favour a single banner, which also matches the ad unit already created on 24 Nov 2026: "Vitals - History banner" (`ca-app-pub-2087846699268567/4316201171`), under App ID `ca-app-pub-2087846699268567~7523537181`. The AdMob app is still "Getting ready", so ad serving stays limited until it is approved.

| Design choice | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Format | One anchored adaptive banner. No interstitial, app-open, native or rewarded ads in v1.1 |
| Where | History and Charts screens only, anchored above the bottom navigation with clear spacing (>8dp) from navigation and any Add button |
| Never | Add/edit reading, result/flag screen, reminders, onboarding, profile management, PDF preview/share, Settings, any alert |
| Personalization | Non-personalized ads for all users. No `keywords`, `contentUrl` or custom targeting, and no screen names that reveal conditions |
| Content controls | Max ad content rating G or PG. Block health, pharma, supplement, dating and gambling categories in AdMob |
| Consent | UMP flow on every launch (`requestConsentInfoUpdate` → `loadAndShowConsentFormIfRequired` → check `canRequestAds()`), then `MobileAds.instance.initialize()`, then load. "Privacy options" button in Settings when required. GDPR/TCF and US-states (GPP/RDP) messages configured in AdMob Privacy & messaging |
| Code structure | One `AdService` behind an interface, gated by an `isAdFree` entitlement. The readings/repository layer has no import path to the ad SDK |
| Large text | At 200% font scale, the banner must not cover or squeeze the navigation bar. Test it |
| Testing | Google test ad units only during development. Never tap live ads |

The consent sequence follows Google's Flutter guidance, including the rule that `canRequestAds()` is false until the consent update has run ([AdMob Flutter privacy](https://developers.google.com/admob/flutter/privacy)). Calling `MobileAds.instance.initialize()` only after the consent check is standard Google sample practice. The quick-start page does not state this order. UMP is needed even for a Pakistan-first app because Play distributes globally, and diaspora caregivers in the UK or the EU trigger the GDPR and TCF rules. Under US state privacy laws, a Restricted Data Processing (RDP) signal makes AdMob stop serving ads based on a user's past behaviour ([AdMob RDP](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9561022)). Pakistan itself has no enacted data protection law yet; only PECA's criminal provisions apply ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=PK)). Building to the GDPR standard now protects against a future law. The plugin is google_mobile_ads **9.2.0**, which needs Flutter 3.38.1+, so the current toolchain works ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/google_mobile_ads); [quick start](https://developers.google.com/admob/flutter/quick-start)). Two details are unconfirmed and need checking against the plugin API docs before coding: the exact per-request non-personalized flag in the Flutter API, and the minimum Android SDK. The research notes did not cover rewarded ads in depth, so they are deferred. If they are ever added, they should unlock extras, never the doctor report.

The paperwork is the larger job. Adding the SDK turns Data safety from "no data shared" into **collected and shared**. The data types are Device or other IDs (advertising ID, app set ID), App interactions, Diagnostics, and approximate location from IP, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, all encrypted in transit ([Mobile Ads SDK disclosure](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure); [Data safety help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)). Health readings stay "collected, not shared". The AD_ID permission merges automatically from play-services-ads for API 33+ targets ([Advertising ID policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248)). The Ads declaration in App content must be set to "contains ads", and the new Data safety answers and the ads build should go to review together. The privacy policy, shared with Medicine Reminder and currently stating there are no ad SDKs, must say which app shows ads. It should name Google AdMob, list the SDK's data, explain consent and how to reset the advertising ID, and state plainly that blood pressure, glucose, SpO2, pulse and weight readings are never sent to advertisers or used to choose ads ([Play Health Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511)). The app-ads.txt crawler uses the exact hostname of the developer website on the Play listing. homilabs.org already lists the publisher, but that does not cover the Vitals listing. The prepared file, with the line `google.com, pub-2087846699268567, DIRECT`, must be served at **https://livehealthy.homilabs.org/app-ads.txt**, which returned 404 on 24 Nov 2026. Verification can take up to 24 hours ([AdMob app-ads.txt](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9363762)).

## Revenue per 1,000 daily users stays in the tens of dollars

Google does not publish country eCPMs (eCPM is revenue per 1,000 ad impressions), so every figure here is an estimate. Illustrative 2026 benchmarks put Pakistan, a Tier-3 ad market, at **$0.08–0.14 for anchored adaptive banners** and $1–2 for interstitials ([RevenueLab](https://www.revenuelab.fyi/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)). One South Asian Flutter developer reports Pakistani banners below $0.15 ([Medium](https://ottomancoder.medium.com/flutter-app-monetization-admob-vs-applovin-max-in-2026-with-real-numbers-from-south-asian-b428eee10196)). The HomiLabs account's recent eCPM of about **$0.40** across ~40 apps sets a reasonable upper bound, since it likely includes better formats and richer countries. Non-personalized serving will pull Vitals lower still.

| Scenario per 1,000 DAU | Assumptions | Est. revenue / month |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 4,500 banner impressions/day at $0.10 eCPM | ~$14 |
| Account-average | Same impressions at $0.40 eCPM | ~$54 |
| With one capped interstitial (not recommended) | + 1,000/day at ~$1.50 | +~$45 |

These are roughly **$0.0005–0.002 per daily user per day**. At that level, a cheap one-time "Remove ads" purchase could earn more than the ads from engaged caregivers, though conversion in Pakistan is unknown. Play payments there rely on cards and carrier billing, carrier billing was disrupted in 2022, and its current status is unverified ([The News](https://thenews.com.pk/print/1014814-dcb-crisis-google-changes-payment-mechanism-to-purchase-apps)). Diaspora children paying with foreign cards are a plausible but unmeasured segment. The case against ads deserves a hearing. The "Contains ads" label and new data-sharing lines may cost more trust among health users than $15–55 a month is worth. The interstitial line in the table shows the temptation, roughly doubling revenue, and its cost: an interstitial lands in the policy grey zone and can block an elderly user from seeing a high reading's confirmation. The banner-only design limits the trust cost, and the owner's own AdMob reports 4–8 weeks after launch are the only real data for deciding whether to keep it. Mediation (adding other ad networks behind AdMob) should wait. Each added network means another SDK in Data safety, app-ads.txt and the consent setup.

## Conclusion

Vitals' best route to growth in Pakistan is to become the record a family brings to the doctor. That means a bilingual app that produces a guideline-shaped 7-day BP summary, shares it through WhatsApp, and later shows medicine adherence beside BP from the sibling app. No Play competitor combines all of this today, and the only Urdu rival is weeks old. Advertising is a minor part of the plan. Its main value is to normalise a one-time "Remove ads" purchase and keep the portfolio consistent, not to fund development.

The ads work also has a benefit beyond revenue. Building the ad layer now, with non-personalized serving and a code-level wall between readings and the ad SDK, is the same evidence Play will ask for when Health Connect arrives in v1.2. Done carefully, v1.1's compliance work prepares that review. Done carelessly, for example with an interstitial after Save or a health keyword in an ad request, it puts at risk both the AdMob account that serves ~40 other apps and the app's standing with Play's health-data rules.
